Model notes

How the forecast works

Target and observed weather

The target is the change in EIA’s Lower 48 working gas inventory between consecutive Friday mornings, measured in billion cubic feet (Bcf). For each week, the model uses NOAA’s population-weighted U.S. heating and cooling degree days (HDD and CDD, 65°F base) from the previous Friday through Thursday. It produces its estimate after the weather week closes and before the storage report. “Henry Hub” identifies the market context. The storage total covers the Lower 48 states.

Five-year baseline and weather adjustment

The baseline is the median change on the closest Friday to the same calendar date in each of the prior five years. A linear model fits the part of historical storage changes left over after that baseline using heating and cooling degree-day differences from their own five-year medians. The component table shows both weather contributions and the fitted intercept. It measures historical associations. Production, exports, outages and storage reclassifications can also influence reported changes.

Historical errors and the 80% interval

Every replay week is forecast using only earlier training weeks, with a minimum of 104 and a maximum of 156. The interval uses an 80th-percentile absolute error from up to 104 previous out-of-sample weeks, after at least 52 exist. The scorecard averages absolute misses on the same eligible weeks for both models. Coverage measures the share of actual changes inside the interval. Historical inputs come from today’s available data vintages and may include revisions. Historical forecasts assume the complete weather week was available before the storage release.

Publication and prospective scoring

The first complete estimate recorded for a target week is frozen. The pipeline checks the EIA’s stated next release and holiday schedule before accepting it. The live score uses the net change in the first EIA weekly release table captured by the pipeline and requires evidence that a successful public Pages deployment contained the identical forecast before the release. A missed release or missing publication evidence leaves a forecast unscored. Later changes to shared historical series can update the replay.
